# History of the Realm
The river of time doth not flow through Faerie like the waters of the Material, the sand within its hourglass falls unpredictably and without witness. This strangeness leaves a realm that is less reliant on the calendar, where season is location and where its most potent figures measure their lives in aeons rather than years. Yet whilst the current of time here may ebb and flow, it remains as linear as anywhere. The events of its history lie scattered in its wake, leaving a tapestry that marks the passage of time through the sequence of story rather than date. 

The earliest days of this realm are not known, its beginning shrouded in mystery. For most, Faerie has always been and shall always be. The earliest stories speak of a vast and untamed wilderness, that stretched beyond the horizon to grasp at the clouds themselves. In those earliest of stories the land was ruled by the four courts of the wyldfey, their ever changing whims guiding the lands through its darker days.

How the cardinal houses came to be is matter of much debate; But there are those who posit that as the world of mortals, sibling as it is to this realm, grew more stable, so did Faerie. Season emerged in the Material, and their living embodiment took corpus in Faerie. The arrival of the sidhe and houses of season took the wyldcourts by surprise, they were little prepared for their imperial natures and even less so for the determination by which they would enact it. War as we now know it still had yet to took root, and instead the wyldcourts merely faded into obscurity, having no want for the power the sidhe hungered for.

As the realm settled into the rule of the sidhe, seasons washed over its wilds, and the barriers between it and other worlds seems to wax and wane. Mortals began wandering beyond the veils of their own worlds, like ducklings haplessly falling between cracks in the flagstone of reality. Most went unnoticed by the fey, and few if any ever returned home.
 
With the wyldcourts conquered, the houses had little left but to war within themselves. The cardinal houses each formed faction, Summer and Winter first before further fractioning created Spring and Autumn, and the thunder of civil war rumbled on the horizon. It is at this moment that a new power had begun to emerge. The House of Blight, straying from the dominion of the cardinal four, was growing in the gloomiest corners of the realms. Unlike its siblings Blight sought no power over the natural cycle of the realm, it meant to consume the cycle entirely. The cardinal houses stifled their petty grievances, and called for total war on the House of Blight, such was its threat. The war that followed decimated the realm, and left many legendary figures of the realm decaying in the mud to be consigned to the annals of history. This war to end all wars culminated not in a defeat of the pox house, but rather in its banishment. A protective ring of menhir stones were erected, a perimeter that stretched thousands of miles from the Giantscairn mountains to the Sea of Mordu.
 
It was not long after this hell that the first foundations of Avalon started to be laid down. Mortals had continued to seep into the realm during the blight war for Faerie, only now they had found leadership and structure under the banner of a mortal king lost within this realm, Pendragon. So whilst the alien world about them made war with decay itself, the mortals continued to place one stone atop another until hovels became huts, huts became houses, and houses become the free city of Avalon. 

Meanwhile the bliss of bitter victory, that had lulled the cardinal houses into diplomacy for some time, was wearing off. Arguments had grown more heated, and the ruling houses now looked upon the finite space of a once endless realm, with each looking to carve out a stake in it lest the others take their place. Civil war began, a war that has continued through ages, a war that still burns hot in the hearts of much of the realm. This, is where we find the Faerie today; torn asunder by the constant flux of combat. Skirmishes on borders, political assassinations at court, threats and declarations that constantly shake the stability of lives of common folk.
