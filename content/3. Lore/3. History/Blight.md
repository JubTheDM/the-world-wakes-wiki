The mouldering decay of a forgotten land sprawls to the endless corners of a conquered realm. This was once Faerie, once a far-reaching world that stretched to the horizon and beyond. Now it rots like the corpse of a murdered memory, its flesh wilted into horrid sinew, its bones bleached and scattered for carrion. The lands of Blight are all that remains of the realm beyond the menhirs, a graveyard of legends where all that once was has come to fester under the roots of an eternal sickness. Great fortresses crumble under fell tendrils, forests wither to its sickly orange glow, peoples and beasts roam its wilderness hollow of mind or spirit. Blight is not just disease, nor is it the natural decay of the dead that feeds the cycles of life; it is the most grim corruption of vitality itself. 

Blight does not return that which it consumes into the systems of nature; it holds onto it like a parasitic infection seeking to make all that lives a vessel for its multiplication. It does not simply kill, it takes possession, and it seeks not to become part of the wider world but to infest it entirely. The air in this place is thick with the smog of putrefaction, the ground littered with the rotting vestiges of what once was called life. Even the structures that remain now stand like ruined tombstones, epitaphs to the death of civilisation. Beneath it all thrums a vile heartbeat, a unity that connects these horrid roots under a single-minded will. This is no mere plague; it is an apocalypse given thought and driven by avarice

Blight is more than a place, more than mere disease; it is the hand through which the most malevolent of evils does its work within the realm of Faerie. To wander into its bounds, beyond the protection of the menhirs and into the badlands, is to walk headfirst into death itself. Nothing remains in this place that once lived; all has been condemned to the purgatory of eternal decay.
