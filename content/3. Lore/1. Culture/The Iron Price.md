Iron is a precious resource in the realm of Faerie due to its rarity and potency (particularly when made into coldiron) when used against the realm's natural inhabitants. Iron does not occur naturally anywhere in the lands of the fey (save for the scales of iron wyrms), and so cannot be mined or otherwise extracted, nor can magic produce iron or any iron derivatives there; a fact most fey are grateful for. As a result, any iron within Faerie has been brought into the realm from the mortal plane upon the person of anyone who tumbled between the veil of their own world into the otherworld. This simple but foundational fact of the realm has a big impact on campaigns run within Faerie, as those adventurers carrying iron are likely going to be relentlessly targeted by either Peregrine Knights of Avalon seeking to confiscate and protect the iron on their person, or by fey who wish to destroy the iron (and likely its wielder) out of disgust and well-placed fear.

## Coldiron
It is no secret that those born of Faerie are particularly vulnerable to attacks made with weapons of iron, but in truth simple smithed iron will provide no real advantage over fey. It is coldiron that is needed, a legendary and exceptionally rare form of iron that takes its recipe from the tales of old:

> [!quote] The First Iron
> *"And so he wrought a weapon that cut through the otherkin like a mighty galley through rough water. Its' birth was smelted in spring, its blade forged in the heats of summer, its metal tempered in the autumn, and its fires quenched in the ice of winter."* 
>
>Morta Chronicles, Archive of Avalon

Such metal would be impossible to forge in the world of mortals, the seasons separated by months as they are. But in the realm of Faerie, at the heart of the free city of Avalon where the surrounding borders of each cardinal kingdom meet in conjunction, there lies a great forge named the Iron Altar. It is at the altar that sacred smiths work whatever iron they can find into coldiron to arm the knights of Avalon with. 

## Dur Steel
Dur steel, sometimes called caladur, is a dull steel- like metal that is the predominant material used in the creation of weapons and armour by the houses of Faerie. It is non-ferrous, and thus easily usable by the fey, as well as naturally occurring in abundance particularly in the coast cliffs of the Pengallon region of Summer. Dur is prized for its resilient properties, and natural reactivity to magic. 

Dur steel is used in the production of armour, weapons, and even as a powdered component in spellcasting. Weapons and armour made of dur have
the following properties:

- **Resilient.** Dur weapons and armour are immune to the effects of corrosion, wear or aging. 
- **Dur Armour.** Whilst wearing armour made of dur, any creature that hits you with a melee attack takes force damage equal to the highest level concentration spell currently cast on you. 
- **Dur Weapons.** The first time you hit a creature with a dur weapon on a turn, you deal additional force damage equal to the highest level concentration spell currently cast on you. 
- **Powdered Dur.** See the previous section on Adventuring Gear for information on the uses and costs of powdered dur.

## Menhir Bronze
The elder stories speak of weapons from the days of near prehistory, before coldiron, before the walls of Avalon were built, and before the menhir stones stood guard around the realm. These crude bronze blades were blessed by witches of old to face an ancient enemy, the blight. None now know where these weapons went. Some say they are lost, others that they were smelted down into other things once they were no longer needed, but there are a few that maintain that each weapon was placed into one of the menhir stones to be drawn only when need was most dire.
