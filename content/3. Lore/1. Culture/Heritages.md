The realm of Faerie is home to a diverse collection of unique cultures and lineages, born not only of a place and a people but also emerging from events of magic or time spent in the world beyond the mortal. Some of these lineages are likely born as they are, whereas others might have started as one thing and later become another, such as the Hagborn. As such all races and lineages are described in the following section as a heritages, to encompass the many ephemeral ways in which a player character might start as or later become one of the presented options.

---
## Sidhe 
Faerie is home to many fey peoples of many shapes and sizes. Some of these peoples are the ancestors of or descended from lineages you may recognise. Collectively they are referred to as sidhe (SHEE), and make up most everyday people you will encounter within Faerie. Traditionally Sidhe appear as elves, gnomes, dwarves, halflings, though they regard themselves and each other as part of the same lineage regardless of appearance.

## Etterkin
There are many cultures that measure the mark of a man on his ability to work raw material into something beautiful or functional; But none quite like the etterkin. Their more monstrous appearance and tendency to live within the forgotten places of the world could have easily given rise to grim legends shared by folk eager to whisper ignorant untruths about anything vaguely sinister, perhaps some in the mortal realm still do, yet none persist in the realm of Faerie; For in Faerie it is rarely the look of one that garners fable, more often it is their deeds and nature. Much that looks fairer is in fact fouler, and much that looks fouler is, at heart, fairer. The etterkin are perhaps the finest example of such a principle, for while no tales are told of their fairness many are spoken of the beauty they are able to weave with nought but the silken strands strewn from their wrists
#### Foul Places of Fair Folk
Stories speak of a time when etterkin walked the realms openly, a time when master craftsmen sold silken wears to high rulers of the kingdoms. Etterkin were never considered a "common" sight, but neither would the presence of spiderkin be cause for question. Alas, these are but stories of history, the realm now walked is not one in which etterkin are well known. The reason for this shift is uncertain, the details dependant on who is asked, but for one reason or another the etterkin receded into their caverns away from the politics and turmoil of the world around them. Few outside their kin know of these places, hidden as they are in the deepest and darkest corners of the land. Be it in the bows ancient forests, or in the shadowy caverns oft assumed to be the lairs of terrible beasts, the etterkin now live an isolated existence in towns woven of silk, away from the troubles of the world

#### A Place in Legend
In a land of war and fable, many legends persist. Some are mighty warriors and ancient beasts, some are of tragedy and the folly of foolish rulers, and some are of the craftsmen who lent their skill to the artefacts that fuelled these tales. It is in the latter of these legends that the silk folk most often appear. Their natural talent for weaving is spoken of with an awe that is more often reserved for the magical and mystic. Be it the silk weave of armour worn by a hero of legend, or the woven grip of an archaic sword, it is evident for all the mark left by such people on the annals of history. That is not to say that all etterkin pursue such mastery of craft, for they are as individual in their pursuits as any people or culture, rather that within all etterkin resides an innate ability to visualise and create works that would be impossible for the untrained minds of most others.

## Cairndwin
Whispers speak of fey who dwell deep in the barrow downs, beneath the valley of hills formed long ago above ancient cairns. Within these stone tombs lost to memory and buried beneath earth reside not only the long dead forgotten, but a yet still living people. These self adjudicated custodians have grown accustomed to dwelling in places of darkness and death, and watching over those buried has become their sacred duty. Folk say their proximity to death has blessed them with long life, and imbued them with an unusual tether to the veil beyond; allowing them to barter and exchange the essence of their own vitality into feats of will, of strength, and even of magic.
#### Honoured Caretakers
In times forgotten, when those honoured in spirit passed on from life into the inky beyond, they were buried in sacred cairns. They were joined in burial by their riches, their stories, their armaments and armour, and by custodians who were tasked with seeing safe their passage into eternity. To be cairndwin in those years meant to hold a sacred office, to be charged with keeping safe the most important dead so that their journey to the afterlife was secured. Over time these cairns grew expansive, filled with warriors, poets, kings and queens. So too did the number of those trusted to watch over them, enough that eventually the living within the cairns nearly numbered equal to the dead. They made homes, fostered communities, all the while sheltered away from the light of day or influence of their original homes. Eventually burial cairns fell from fashion, and their caretakers similarly forgotten. But whilst the realm may have forgotten, the cairndwin themselves have not; They remain stalwart in their duties, honoured by their charge, ever watchful over the graves of those who rest in death within the prestige earned in life.
#### At Peace With Death
The cairndwin were once sidhe, but their ancient promise has long since left them without the kiss of sunlight or breath of fresh air. They have spent an aeon living beneath the burial stone, steeped in darkness and death. They are changed now, no longer recognisable as they were, adapting to their environment but also saturated in the strange magics held within such places. They are not undead, not twisted or necrotic, but instead share a kinship with it. Whilst the vitality of life still beats within them, it has become less anchored, fluctuating like a candle flame. This tethered separation of vitality from body leaves the cairndwin with a less rigid connection to their own life force, allowing them to manipulate, focus, and even expend it to achieve feats that would appear impossible to most.
#### Forgotten Treasures
Seeing a cairndwin walking the realm above is a rare thing indeed, but grows more common as the age marches on. Some are forced out as their home cairns fall to ruin or corruption, others willed forward by an urgency to witness the world known only to them in story. Whatever the reason, they walk the world an oddity to the ignorant and relic to the wizened. Some even say that when the custodians of the dead walk the land, their charges won't be long to follow...

## Hagborn
Folk have long speculated on the methods by which hags reproduce. Tales speak of strange rituals,
mortal men taken by their trickery, and even of them being born from beasts themselves. All of these tales speak some truth and some lies. The nature of hag is something as fickle and changing as the elder magics they revel in. But there is a truth to the tales that those who narrowly avoid the fate of becoming their twisted kin know all too well.
#### Stolen Childhood
Pilfered away in the dead of night while they slept in their cribs, pulled below the seemingly shallow waters of a meandering river, beckoned to wander deep into the forest never to be seen again. What hags intend do with the children they steal very much depends on the hag, and their reasons for such treachery. Eating them, sacrificing them to the old magic, using them in strange ancient blood rituals. The hagborn were all once children of other lineages; Elves, Humans, Halflings. Children stolen from their homes, whisked away by these treacherous creatures to the lands of Faerie, to become something else entirely
#### Ritual Gone Wrong
Most hagborn are created in a way most literal to their name. Children taken by hags to create a new generation of twisted crone. Only female children are taken for this purpose, stolen to be grown like seeds in a sickened garden. Few hagborn are male, though some do exist. These hagborn are children who escaped rituals before they were entirely taken by the old magic and snuffed out entirely. They are still changed by the magic, but not lost to it entirely. There are even stories of tenacious children cutting themselves from the bellies of voracious hags, emerging forever changed. These are the lucky few, the survivors, now stranded in a realm not of their own to find their way without guidance or explanation; driven only by the same tenacity and fortune that spared them a fate far more wicked.
#### The Old Magic
Regardless of their exact origins, all hagborn have been touched in part by the old magic; Ancient elder magics long since forgotten by the lands beyond Faerie. This magic is so old, so ancient and deep, that even to those considered masters it can seem fickle and untameable. Hagborn have this magic within them, allowing them to perform strange feats reminiscent of their rotten beginnings. This magic doesn’t often appear until early adulthood, manifesting slowly as the hagborn settles into the stability of adulthood. To many, even in Faerie, this can be seen as an ill-omen. It can be difficult to tell if a hagborn will continue to change into a fully fledged crone, or cease with their sense of self still intact. As such many shun them, or attempt to use them and their connection to the old magic in their own twisted machinations.
#### Changed By Tragedy
The nature of a hagborn's origin is often unique to the hagborn, and to the hag they are born of, as well as the exact nature of the magic they survived. Some hagborn may develop skin of hues reminiscent of their magical progenitor, others might smell of things distinct to their hag's home. There are some characteristics that seem to be present across most hagborn; Namely that hagborn tend to have longer limbs or fingers than their mortal counterparts. This isn’t always obvious, but it doesn’t take too keen an eye to notice the gangliest of folk sticking out a head's height above those around them. Such things have at times led to the vilification of the tall, fear of the unknown once again ruling the ignorant.
