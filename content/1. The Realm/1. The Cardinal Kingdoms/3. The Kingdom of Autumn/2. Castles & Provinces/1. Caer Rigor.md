Rising from a lone peak in a nest of earth, the rocky fingers of Rigor Henge rise like a great stone hand towards the heavens themselves. Few sights compare to the humble majesty of Caer Rigor; its appearance alone seems to impart a sense of sacred tradition. Approaching the city from its southern road means entering from the Hollow Gate, carved into the earth of the peaked hilltop itself and watched by the towers that loom down from the hilltop above. Beyond the gate is a crossroads of streets carved from stone, lit by the glow of hearth and candlelight that casts stark shadows across the carven stone ceiling above. This space within the stone is but a small section of the city, but the reverence with which it was constructed harkens more to an ancient mausoleum than to its real purpose. Even the columns carved into its corners and edges are adorned with faces of figures forgotten to time, who watch ever stoic as the bustle of civilisation moves beneath them. 

The wider city emerges upon leaving these vast candlelit halls, but not the throne of this place. The throne lies above, at the end of a winding pathway of stone staircases and hallways that carve through the peak as it rises towards the great Rigor Henge at its zenith. This is Caer Rigor, a network of chambers connected by pillared hallways and staircases that wind through the hollow of the peak, is unlike any other in [[The Realm of Faeries|Faerie]], and its rugged yet careful construction tells a tale of its people far clearer than painting or poem ever could.
